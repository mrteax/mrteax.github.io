# Shanghai → France, 25 Sep – 7 Oct 2026: air, rail and ground transport research

**Research date: 24 August 2026.** Audience: Chinese travellers using a France-issued Schengen visa. The schedule requires arrival in Shanghai on 7 October 2026, so the long-haul return must leave France on 6 October.

Every claim below is tagged:

- **[V]** Verified against a primary or authoritative source (airline site, SNCF, service-public.fr, VINCI Autoroutes, State Council, Constitutional Council coverage).
- **[S]** Secondary source only (schedule aggregators, travel media, operator marketing pages). Directionally reliable, but confirm before paying.
- **[E]** Estimate or inference by me. Not a quoted figure.

## 0. Three date facts that change the whole plan

| Date | Day of week | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25 Sep 2026 | **Friday** | Air France's new daytime PVG–CDG flight runs Tue/Thu/Sat, so it does **not** operate on 25 Sep. |
| 6 Oct 2026 | **Tuesday** | Air France's daytime CDG–PVG flight runs Mon/Wed/Fri, so it does **not** operate on 6 Oct. |

**[V]** The State Council's official 2026 holiday notice (国办发明电〔2025〕7号, 4 Nov 2025) sets National Day as **1–7 October 2026 (7 days)**, not 1–8 October, with 20 September (Sun) and 10 October (Sat) as make-up working days. It also sets **Mid-Autumn as 25–27 September (3 days)**, separate from National Day. Source: <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202511/content_7047090.htm>, mirrored at <https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202511/05/content_30113226.html>.

Two consequences travellers should absorb now:

- The **outbound date, 25 September, is the first day of the Mid-Autumn holiday.** It is not a quiet shoulder-season Friday; it is the start of a three-day national break. Outbound demand and fares will be elevated.
- The **return date, 7 October, is the final day of Golden Week** — the single heaviest inbound travel day of the Chinese autumn. See §4.

### What actually operates on Friday 25 September

- ❌ **AF117** — Tue/Thu/Sat only.
- ✅ **AF111** 21:30 → 05:55+1 (26 Sep)
- ✅ **MU553** 00:15 → 06:30 same day
- ✅ **MU569** 12:55 → 19:15 same day

**[E] Recommendation for the outbound.** For travellers seeking lower fatigue or child-friendly timings, **MU569 (12:55 → 19:15)** is the standout: a civilised midday departure from Shanghai, a 12h20m flight, and arrival in Paris at 19:15 in time for a hotel and a night's sleep — no red-eye, no 00:15 check-in, no jet-lagged 06:00 arrival with nowhere to go. Travellers able to shift the outbound to **Saturday 26 September** can use AF117 (10:30 → 19:00, A350-900), which gives the same day-flight shape on a newer aircraft.

The least comfortable option is MU553: a 00:15 departure means being at Pudong around 21:30 on 24 September, then landing at 06:30 and facing a full day with no hotel check-in.

### The Russian airspace penalty — this is the real story in the table

**[V] Air France's PVG–CDG block times are ~2h10m longer than China Eastern's on the identical city pair.** This is arithmetic from the published schedules above, not an opinion: Shanghai is UTC+8, Paris is UTC+2 during the summer season, a 6-hour gap.

- AF111: 21:30 → 05:55+1 = 8h25m on the clock + 6h = **14h25m**
- MU553: 00:15 → 06:30 = 6h15m on the clock + 6h = **12h15m**

**[V] The cause is Russian airspace.** Russia banned EU carriers from its airspace in February 2022 in retaliation for EU sanctions; Chinese carriers retain access. Air France-KLM CEO Ben Smith stated publicly in October 2025 that skirting Russia "adds between two and two-and-a-half hours to flights between Europe and Asia," and that Chinese airlines face no such detour. Reuters via <https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/air-france-russia-china-six/>; see also <https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/159687-af-klm-lufthansa-ask-eu-to-ban-russia-transit-for-rivals> and Air France-KLM's Greater China GM on the same point at <https://www.caixinglobal.com/2024-06-27/russia-detour-hinders-revival-of-european-carriers-china-flights-executive-says-102210596.html>.

**[E]** The practical read is simple: choosing China Eastern buys back roughly two hours of sitting in a seat in each direction — about four hours over the round trip. That is a genuine comfort argument for travellers seeking lower fatigue, not a rounding error. The counter-argument is product: AF117/AF112 are A350-900s, which are newer and quieter than China Eastern's 777-300ERs. **[S]** Aircraft assignments per AeroRoutes and Air France as cited above.

## 3. Nice → CDG on 6 October, and the connection question

### The NCE–CDG market

**[S]** Two carriers fly NCE→CDG: **Air France** (arriving **Terminal 2F**) and **easyJet** (arriving **Terminal 2D**). **Transavia France flies Nice→Paris Orly, not CDG** — so Transavia is irrelevant to a CDG connection. **[S]** Frequency is roughly **9 nonstop departures per day** on the NCE→CDG pair; earliest departure **06:00**; block time **1h35m–1h40m**; all departures from Nice **Terminal 2**. <https://www.flightsfrom.com/NCE-CDG>, <https://www.kayak.com/flight-routes/Nice-Cote-d-Azur-NCE/Paris-Charles-de-Gaulle-CDG>

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AF7321** | Daily | **06:00** | 07:35 (2F) | A320 |
| **AF7301** | Daily | **09:05** | 10:40 (2F) | A220-300 |

**[S]** <https://info.flightmapper.net/flight/Air_France_AF_7321> and <https://info.flightmapper.net/flight/Air_France_AF_7301>. Additional daily AF rotations AF7311 and AF7313 also run NCE→CDG into 2F with 1h35m block times (<https://aviability.com/en/flight/af7311-air-france>, <https://aviability.com/en/flight/af7313-air-france>) — exact departure times for 6 October need checking on airfrance.com.

**[S] Worth noting:** AF7313 is already sold by China Eastern as **MU1516** under the SkyTeam codeshare (<https://aviability.com/en/flight/af7313-air-france>). That is direct evidence that a single MU-ticketed NCE–CDG–PVG itinerary is constructible.

### Is a same-day NCE → CDG → PVG connection safe?

**[E] Yes, comfortably — but only if it is one ticket, and the margin depends entirely on which long-haul you pick.**

| Domestic leg | Long-haul | Layover at CDG | Verdict **[E]** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AF7321 06:00 → 07:35 | MU554 13:25 | **5h50m** | Very safe; arguably wasteful |
| AF7301 09:05 → 10:40 | MU554 13:25 | **2h45m** | **Recommended sweet spot** |
| AF7321 06:00 → 07:35 | MU570 21:20 | ~13h45m | Absurd; don't |
| Any morning NCE flight | AF116 23:30 | 14h+ | Take an evening NCE flight instead |

**AF7301 → MU554 with 2h45m is the recommendation.** It clears any plausible minimum connecting time with a large cushion, avoids a 04:00 wake-up in Nice, and still lands in Shanghai at 07:00 on 7 October.

### Terminals: 2F → 2E, and the border crossing nobody plans for

**[V/S]** Air France's Nice flights land at **Terminal 2F**, which is the **Schengen** hall. Both China Eastern long-hauls and Air France's long-hauls depart from **Terminal 2E**, the **non-Schengen** hall. (2F/2E terminal assignments per FlightMapper and FlyTeam, cited above; the 2F = Schengen / 2E = non-Schengen split per <https://traveltipsparis.com/cdg-terminal-2e-to-2f/>.)

**[S]** The transfer is airside via a dedicated connecting corridor between 2E's K/L gate areas and 2F. Going 2F → 2E you must clear **exit border control** (leaving Schengen) and **be re-screened at security**. <https://traveltipsparis.com/cdg-terminal-2e-to-2f/>

**[S] One more current wrinkle:** with the EU **Entry/Exit System (EES)** now live at French borders, non-EU nationals face a biometric step at the border. One 2026 guide estimates 10–15 extra minutes on connections involving passport control (<https://privatedrive.co/en/blog/connecting-flights-via-paris-cdg-transit-transfer-guide>). **[E]** I could not confirm the exit-side impact for Chinese passport holders from an official ADP or French Interior Ministry page, so treat this as a reason to prefer the 2h45m layover over anything tighter — not as a hard number.

### Official minimum connecting time at CDG — what I can and cannot verify

This is where I have to be careful, because the internet is full of confidently contradictory numbers.

**[V] What Paris Aéroport itself publishes.** Its own airline-facing CDG customer guide gives an MCT table sourced from OAG, and the relevant figures are:

- Within Terminal 2 (halls A/B/C/D/E/F/G): **90 minutes**
- Between two Terminal 2 halls: **90 minutes**
- Between Terminal 1 and Terminal 2: 120 minutes

with the explicit note: *"These times are applicable to any type of connection (Schengen/Schengen, Schengen/International, International/Schengen and International/International)."*
Source: <https://www.parisaeroport.fr/docs/default-source/professionnel-fichiers/services-aux-compagnies-aeriennes/edito_information_paris_cdg.pdf>

**⚠️ Caveat I must flag: that table is labelled "source: OAG – April 2011."** It is published by the airport, but it is dated. I could not retrieve a current official ADP MCT table — parisaeroport.fr blocked automated access during this research.

**[V] Why the airport figure is not the operative number anyway.** OAG explains that airlines file their own MCT exceptions on top of the airport standard, and that these carrier-specific values are what GDSs and booking engines actually use. OAG states that **CDG carries roughly 10,160 individual carrier exceptions** — more than four times Heathrow's. Air France, as the CDG hub carrier, files its own shorter minimums for its own connections. Source: <https://www.oag.com/blog/minimum-connection-times-insiders-guide>

**[S] Unverified figures circulating for Schengen → non-Schengen, same terminal at CDG:** 60 minutes (<https://travelvient.com/guides/minimum-connection-time-cdg-2026/>), 50 minutes (<https://privatedrive.co/en/blog/connecting-flights-via-paris-cdg-transit-transfer-guide>), ~70 minutes as a same-terminal recommendation (<https://layoverindex.com/cdg/>). **These do not agree with each other and none cites an airline tariff.** I am not going to pick one and present it as the official number.

**[E] The honest bottom line, which is also the useful one:** you do not need to know Air France's filed MCT, because **you should not build this connection anywhere near it.** With a 2h45m layover you are roughly double even the most conservative published airport standard, and the question becomes moot.

### Why a single through ticket matters more than the MCT

**[V] Air France states the mechanism plainly on its own connections page:**

> *"The travel times between your 2 flights are taken into account when you buy a connecting ticket on our website. In case your flight arrives late and the minimum connecting time cannot be met, the airline responsible for the delay will offer you a rerouting solution."*
>
> *"If you purchase your flights separately, you must ensure that you have enough time between each flight to make your connection. If your flight is delayed, the airline operating your flight will not be responsible for your rerouting, and will not necessarily offer you an alternative solution."*

Source: <https://wwws.airfrance.fr/en/information/aeroport/conseils-correspondances-paris-cdg-ory>

**[E] For travellers**, booking NCE–CDG–PVG as one multi-city / open-jaw ticket on Air France or China Eastern buys four distinct things:

1. **Protected connection.** If the Nice flight is late and the long-haul is missed, the carrier rebooks affected travellers at its own cost. On separate tickets, a missed long-haul can mean replacement one-way tickets bought at the counter during Golden Week — potentially a five-figure CNY event for a multi-person booking.
2. **Baggage checked through** from Nice to Pudong. There is no reclaiming luggage at CDG, re-entering the terminal or re-checking, which is a major practical gain for visitors seeking lower walking intensity.
3. **The booking engine validates the connection** against the filed MCT, so an illegal connection cannot be sold.
4. **The Schengen exit is handled airside** on the transfer circuit rather than by exiting into the public terminal and coming back through.

**[E] Practical booking note:** the natural constructions are (a) all-Air France — AF NCE→CDG plus AF116, one carrier, maximum simplicity, but lands Shanghai 18:00 on 7 Oct; or (b) an AF-operated NCE→CDG plus MU554, priced as a single SkyTeam itinerary, landing 07:00 on 7 Oct. Option (b) is better on timing, and the existence of the AF7313/MU1516 codeshare shows the inventory exists. Book it on **ceair.com** or **airfrance.com.cn** as a multi-city search (PVG→CDG out, NCE→PVG back), not as two separate bookings.

**⚠️ Do not connect off easyJet.** easyJet lands in Terminal 2D, does not interline with Air France or China Eastern, and cannot be sold on a through ticket. A missed long-haul would be entirely the traveller's responsibility.

### The low-risk alternative worth pricing

**[S]** There are direct TGVs from **Nice Ville to Paris Gare de Lyon in 5h36m–5h55m, roughly 7 direct services a day**, first departure 05:58, per SNCF Connect (<https://www.sncf-connect.com/en-en/train/timetables/nice/paris>) and Trainline (<https://www.thetrainline.com/en-us/train-times/nice-ville-to-paris-gare-de-lyon>).

**[E]** Travelling Nice→Paris by TGV on **5 October**, sleeping near CDG, and flying out relaxed on the 6th eliminates the connection risk entirely, removes a flight from the itinerary, and is far kinder to visitors seeking lower walking intensity than a 06:00 airport start. It costs one hotel night. For travellers with a hard, non-negotiable arrival deadline, this is the option I would price first.

## 4. Fares and booking urgency

I have to be blunt here: **I could not obtain live, date-specific fare quotes for 25 September / 6 October 2026 from an authoritative source.** Airline and OTA booking engines are not retrievable by automated fetch. Everything in this section is therefore **[S]** or **[E]** and must be checked against a live search before any decision.

### What the secondary data suggests

**[S]** A Chinese-language 2026 route analysis puts Shanghai–Paris round-trip economy, all-in, at **CNY 3,500–5,000 in low season** and **CNY 6,500–9,500 in peak season (National Day, Spring Festival, July–August)**, noting that Shanghai is the cheapest of the three mainland gateways thanks to AF/MU competition. <https://www.travelarbitrage.net/blog/china-to-europe-paris-roundtrip-2026/>

**[S]** Air France's own China site displays lead-in PVG–CDG round-trip economy fares from **CNY 5,447** for sample dates in May and June 2026 — i.e. off-peak. <https://www.airfrance.com.cn/zh-cn/从上海到法国的航班>

**[E] My read for these specific dates: expect CNY 7,000–10,000 per adult round-trip, and treat anything under CNY 6,500 as a good deal worth booking on the spot.** Reasoning: the outbound falls on day one of the Mid-Autumn break and the return lands on the final day of Golden Week — both are demand peaks, and they stack. This is an estimate built on the ranges above, not a quote.

**[E] Unit air-budget guidance:** expect CNY 7,000–10,000 per adult round trip. A child fare is typically 75% of the adult fare on long-haul, plus full taxes — **[E]**, verify at booking. Add the NCE–CDG sector per traveller.

### Is the 6–7 October return expensive or sold out?

**[E] Expensive: near-certainly. Sold out: not yet, but the good buckets will be.** The mechanism is specific and worth understanding:

7 October is the last day of a 7-day national holiday **[V]**. Every Chinese traveller who went abroad for Golden Week is trying to be home for work on 8 October. Demand on Europe→China flights arriving 6–7 October is concentrated into a two-day spike. Simultaneously the outbound date sits on day one of Mid-Autumn.

**[E] Booking urgency: high, and the return leg is the binding constraint.** Today is 24 August — six weeks out from the return. For multi-person bookings, the issue is not whether *a* seat exists but whether **all required seats exist in the same fare bucket on the same flight**. Long-haul aircraft typically hold only a handful of seats in the cheapest classes; a larger booking can exhaust a bucket and roll every traveller up to the next price point. On a peak-return date six weeks out, the cheap buckets on MU554 for 6 October are likely already gone.

**[E] Concrete advice:** price it today, book within days, and if all required seats at an acceptable fare are not available on MU554, take AF116 on 6 October rather than moving the date. Also check the **PVG–CDG outbound and the CDG/NCE–PVG return as one multi-city ticket** versus two one-ways — on peak dates the round-trip construction is frequently cheaper, and it delivers the connection protection described in §3.

## 5. French rail (SNCF), 2026 realities

### 5.1 Paris Montparnasse → Tours / Saint-Pierre-des-Corps

**[V]** Two different destinations that matter for planning:

- **Saint-Pierre-des-Corps (SPDC)** is on the high-speed line. Direct TGV inOui from **Paris Montparnasse Hall 1 & 2** takes about **1h00–1h05** (e.g. TGV inOui 8441, 06:20 → 07:21). It is an out-of-town interchange station, ~5 minutes from central Tours.
- **Tours Centre** requires either a TER shuttle from SPDC (5–10 min, connections of 5–20 min) **or** one of the TGV inOui services that runs into Tours itself, taking **1h12–1h27**.

Source: SNCF Connect timetables, <https://www.sncf-connect.com/en-en/train/timetables/paris/tours> and <https://www.sncf-connect.com/train/horaires/paris/tours>

**[V] Frequency:** SNCF Connect reports **24–26 journeys per day** Paris→Tours, first departure ~06:00, last ~22:52, average journey 1h52m (inflated by the TER/OUIGO options), shortest 0h57m. Direct TGV inOui services into Tours appear at roughly 12:24, 16:39, 17:51, 18:25 and 19:25 on the sample dates shown.

**[S] Fares:** SNCF Connect displays lead-in prices from **€30** on TGV inOui; Trainline lists tickets from about **$12 / £11** for the cheapest advance buckets. <https://www.sncf-connect.com/en-en/train/route/paris/tours>, <https://www.thetrainline.com/en-us/train-times/paris-montparnasse-to-tours>

**[E] For multi-person bookings with luggage, book a direct TGV inOui into Tours Centre even if it costs a few euros more and takes 20 minutes longer.** Moving luggage across a platform at SPDC for a 5-minute TER hop, with an 8-minute connection, is exactly the kind of thing that goes wrong for visitors seeking lower walking intensity.

### 5.2 Tours / Saint-Pierre-des-Corps → Avignon: the hard leg

This is the weakest link in the whole rail plan, and the honest answer is more nuanced than "yes there's a direct TGV" or "no there isn't."

**[V] What exists.** There *is* an intersecteur (province-to-province) TGV inOui corridor running **Nantes – Angers – Saumur – Saint-Pierre-des-Corps – Massy TGV – Lyon Part-Dieu – Valence TGV – Avignon TGV – Aix-en-Provence TGV – Marseille**, operated as **TGV inOui 5300 and 5306**. SNCF Connect shows 5306 departing SPDC at 14:27 and reaching Avignon TGV at 18:52 — **4h25m, no change** — on at least some dates. Source: <https://www.sncf-connect.com/en-en/train/timetables/saint-pierre-des-corps/avignon>

**[V] Why you cannot count on it.** On other dates the *same train numbers* terminate or split at **Lyon Part-Dieu**, and SNCF's own itinerary requires a change there with a 36-minute to 1h01m connection before continuing to Avignon. Source (same SNCF Connect pages, plus <https://www.sncf-connect.com/en-en/train/timetables/nantes/marseille>). Wikipedia's survey of long-distance services lists the through **Nantes–Marseille TGV 5306 as running weekly, on Fridays** — 1,163 km, 8 stops, 6h40m. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longest_train_services>

**[S] Commercial planners say "no direct."** Trainline states flatly: *"No, there are no direct train services from St-Pierre-des-Corps to Avignon TGV… a minimum of 1 change,"* fastest 4h31m, ~19 journeys/day. <https://www.thetrainline.com/en/train-times/st-pierre-des-corps-to-avignon-tgv>

**[E] Reconciling this: a through TGV inOui SPDC → Avignon TGV in about 4h25m exists on limited days (Friday appears to be one), and on most other days the journey requires a change at Lyon Part-Dieu and takes 5–6 hours.** Travellers must check their *exact* date on SNCF Connect. If a through service runs, take it; if not, choose between the two routings below.

**Routing A — change at Lyon Part-Dieu (no Paris involved).** SPDC → Massy TGV → Lyon Part-Dieu on one train, then a second TGV to Avignon TGV. **[V]** ~5–6h total per SNCF Connect. **[E]** Advantage: one platform change inside a single station, no metro, no city crossing, luggage stays with you. Disadvantage: long, and a missed connection at Lyon means a long wait.

**Routing B — back through Paris with a station change.** SPDC → Paris Montparnasse (~1h00), cross Paris to Gare de Lyon, then Gare de Lyon → Avignon TGV. **[V]** SNCF Connect itself offers this, e.g. *SPDC 12:25 → Montparnasse 13:36 → [1h02 connection] → Gare de Lyon → Avignon TGV 17:17* — about **4h50m door to door**. **[V]** Avignon Congrès states Paris–Avignon TGV is **2h40m**. <https://avignon-congres-expo.com/congres-et-seminaires/acces/>

**[E] How bad is the Paris station change with luggage? Bad enough that I would avoid it for travellers seeking lower walking intensity.** The specifics:

- **[S]** Metro Montparnasse-Bienvenüe → Gare de Lyon is **18–30 minutes** of actual travel, via Line 4 to Châtelet then Line 14 (one stop), or Line 6 to Bercy then Line 14 (one stop). <https://www.itineraire-metro.fr/subway/trajet-Montparnasse-Bienvenue-vers-Gare+de+Lyon.html>
- **[S] Montparnasse-Bienvenüe is a notoriously punishing station.** ShowMeTheJourney: the Line 4 platforms are reached by *"long passage ways. So long, that despite the travellators, it takes 8–10 minutes to make the transfer… You will experience a 'will I ever get there' feeling and between the travellator and the Metro platform you'll also have to negotiate stairs — so it's awkward if you have luggage."* <https://showmethejourney.com/train-travel-info/countries/france/cities/paris/rail-stations/paris-montparnasse/>
- **[S] Line 14 is the only 100% step-free line on the Paris metro**, with street-to-platform lifts at all 21 stations — which is why both recommended routings end on it. <https://tips4trip.com/5-essential-steps-to-navigate-the-paris-metro-with-luggage/>
- **[S]** The **bus 91** runs directly between the two stations and avoids stairs entirely; ShowMeTheJourney and the Rick Steves forum both flag it as the luggage-friendly option, with the caveat that a city bus may not have space for a multi-person booking's luggage.
- **[S]** Driving time Montparnasse → Gare de Lyon is about **14 minutes** with no traffic; taxi fares are cited around **€20–30**. Add the taxi queue at Montparnasse, which is unpredictable. <https://community.ricksteves.com/travel-forum/to-the-west/58658>, <https://community.ricksteves.com/travel-forum/france/changing-train-stations-in-paris-181597db-0760-4e3f-a33f-dc160a210c8e>

**[E] Verdict:** if travelling via Paris, use taxis and allow a **full 90 minutes** between trains — not the 60 minutes SNCF's own planner will sell. A standard Paris taxi may not fit a multi-person booking plus luggage, so more than one vehicle may be required; the cited unit fare is **€20–30 per taxi**. **[E]** The metro through Montparnasse-Bienvenüe's corridors is not a plan I would endorse for travellers with substantial luggage or lower walking tolerance.

**[E] Overall recommendation for this leg:** check SNCF Connect for a through 5300/5306 on the actual date. If it exists, book it. If not, prefer **Routing A (change at Lyon Part-Dieu)** despite the extra hour — one indoor platform change beats crossing Paris. Consider breaking the journey with a night in Lyon, which turns the worst travel day of the trip into a pleasant one.

### 5.3 Avignon TGV → Nice Ville

**[S] Direct TGV inOui exists and is good: about 3 direct services a day, 2h58m–3h05m**, routed via Marseille Saint-Charles, Toulon, Saint-Raphaël, Cannes and Antibes. Sample direct departures on aggregator timetables: 08:28 → 12:02, 13:04 → 16:02/16:04, 17:04 → 20:03. Non-direct options (usually a change at Marseille) run 3h30m–4h30m and there are 11–18 total journeys per day.

Sources: <https://www.thetrainline.com/en/train-times/avignon-tgv-to-nice>, <https://www.thetrainline.com/en-us/train-times/avignon-tgv-to-nice-ville>, <https://www.rome2rio.com/Train/Avignon-TGV-Station/Nice>, <https://railmonsters.com/r/france/j/avignon-to-nice>

**[S] Fares:** one-way second class from about **€25** for advance buckets, rising to around **€95** for flexible or late-booked tickets. <https://railmonsters.com/r/france/j/avignon-to-nice>

**[E]** Take a **direct** service and accept whatever departure time it forces. Three hours seated beats 3h40m with a change at Marseille Saint-Charles, which is a busy station with a lot of steps.

### 5.4 The Avignon "Virgule" shuttle

**[V]** The **Virgule d'Avignon** (officially the *raccordement de la Courtine*) is a 1.5 km single-track electrified link opened in **2013** connecting **Avignon Centre** station to **Avignon TGV** station. It runs **35 round trips per day Mon–Fri** (1/hour off-peak, 3/hour at peaks) and 30 on weekends, operated as TER PACA. <https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgule_d'Avignon>

**[V] Journey time 4 minutes.** Avignon Congrès adds two details that matter: *"La Virgule circule sur le même quai que les TGV arrivant du nord de la France… il n'y a pas de rupture de charge pour les voyageurs"* — it uses **the same platform** as TGVs arriving from northern France, so there is no schlep between platforms — and the fare can be **included in the SNCF through ticket**, or bought locally **from €1.60**. <https://avignon-congres-expo.com/congres-et-seminaires/acces/>

**[S]** The Festival d'Avignon's visitor page quotes **6 minutes, €1.70, roughly every 30 minutes**, plus bus line 10 as an alternative (20 min, €1.40). <https://festival-avignon.com/en/prepare-your-trip-600>

**[S] Important caveat:** ShowMeTheJourney warns the TER shuttles are *"not specifically timed to meet TGV trains to/from Paris to give optimum connections,"* and that if you booked a ticket only to Avignon TGV you must buy a separate ticket for the shuttle. Gaps of an hour or more can occur. <https://showmethejourney.com/train-travel-info/countries/france/cities/avignon/rail-stations/avignon-tgv/>

**[E] Action:** book the SNCF ticket all the way through to **Avignon Centre**, not to Avignon TGV. It bundles the Virgule, guarantees a sensible connection, and avoids buying a separate ticket on arrival.

### 5.5 Sales window — and yes, the tickets are already on sale

**[V] This is the single most actionable fact in the rail section: TGV inOui tickets for travel from 4 July to 12 December 2026 went on sale on 11 March 2026.** Their entire 25 September – 7 October travel window is therefore **already bookable today**. Source: <https://www.voyagerentrain.fr/ouverture-billets-automne-2026/> and <https://www.voyagerentrain.fr/ouverture-billets-ete-2026/>

**[V] The underlying rule change.** SNCF Voyageurs confirms on its corporate site that **since 11 March 2026, all TGV inOui tickets open for sale six months ahead** (previously four), covering TGV inOui, OUIGO Grande Vitesse and international TGVs. Source: <https://www.groupe-sncf.com/fr/groupe/portrait-entreprise/groupe-societes/sncf-voyageurs/billets-tgv-inoui-reservables-six-mois>

> **⚠️ Documented inconsistency:** SNCF Connect's own FAQ still opens with *"En temps normal, vous pouvez acheter vos billets de train 4 mois à l'avance"* — the pre-March-2026 rule — while listing the new fixed opening dates below it (e.g. **30 September 2026** for travel 13 Dec 2026 – 6 Jan 2027). <https://www.sncf-connect.com/aide/l-ouverture-des-ventes> The corporate announcement is the authoritative statement; the 4-month line appears to be stale text. Intercités (non-TGV) *does* still open 4 months ahead.

**[E] How prices move:** SNCF uses yield management, so the cheapest buckets sell first and fares rise as departure approaches. Having been on sale since 11 March, the cheapest Prems buckets for these dates are likely already gone, but mid-tier fares should still be available. **[V] The de-risking feature:** TGV inOui and Intercités tickets are **modifiable and refundable free of charge up to 7 days before departure** (<https://www.voyagerentrain.fr/ouverture-billets-automne-2026/>). **[E] So the correct move is to book now at today's price and treat the free-change window as an option** — there is no reason to wait, and waiting only costs money.

### 5.6 Booking from China, and the payment problem

**[V] What SNCF Connect accepts:** CB, **Visa**, Visa Electron, **Mastercard**, **American Express**, Maestro, plus PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Chèques-Vacances. Source: <https://www.sncf-connect.com/en-en/help/payment-methods-accepted-pay-your-ticket>

**⚠️ [V] UnionPay is not on that list.** A China-issued UnionPay-only card will not work on SNCF Connect. Travellers need a **dual-currency Visa or Mastercard**, or a workaround.

**[S] The wider, well-documented problem:** SNCF Connect and Ouigo have a long-standing reputation for rejecting non-French cards, driven by **3-D Secure** authentication that many non-European issuers cannot complete. Because the transaction is short-circuited before it reaches the issuing bank, **the bank never sees a declined charge and cannot fix it.** Reports span Brazilian, US, Canadian, Australian and even some EU cards. Sources: <https://minimalist.travel/travel-prep/france-train-tickets-international-credit-card/>, <https://minimalist.travel/transport/trains/sncf-website-problems/>, <https://community.ricksteves.com/travel-forum/france/difficulty-using-credit-card-on-snfc-official-site>

**[E] Diagnostic tip worth passing on:** if payment fails, call the card issuer and ask whether they can *see* the attempted charge. If they cannot, it is 3-D Secure and no amount of arguing with the bank will help — switch payment method.

**Workarounds, in priority order [S]:**

1. **Trainline** (<https://www.thetrainline.com>) — the most commonly recommended fallback. Same SNCF dynamic fares, sometimes better routings, plus a booking fee of roughly 3%. Accepts **PayPal**, which sidesteps card processing entirely. Multiple forum reports describe a card failing on SNCF Connect and succeeding on Trainline minutes later, and PayPal succeeding where both card attempts failed.
2. **Rail Europe** — comparable coverage and international-card friendliness. Charges a **per-order booking fee of €6.95 / £5.95 / US$7.95** (waived under €15/£15/US$15). Accepts Mastercard, Visa, Visa Electron, Maestro, Amex. Does **not** carry OUIGO. <https://minimalist.travel/travel-prep/france-train-tickets-international-credit-card/>
3. **PayPal on any of the above** — the most reliable single fix reported.
4. **Alipay/WeChat Pay are not accepted** by any of these three **[E]** — do not plan around them.

**[E] Recommendation:** try SNCF Connect first (no fee, and its app holds the tickets natively). Have a dual-currency Visa/Mastercard ready. If it fails twice, go straight to Trainline with PayPal rather than fighting it — the ~3% fee is worth far less than the aggravation, and Trainline's support is in English.

### 5.7 Seat reservation, seat choice, family space

**[V] Reservation is mandatory on TGV inOui** — a specific seat is always allocated with the ticket; there is no unreserved travel. <https://www.sncf-connect.com/fr-ch/tgv-inoui>

**[V] How much choice you get depends on class:**

- **2nd class:** you state *preferences* — upper or lower deck (on duplex sets), window or aisle, direction of travel, duo (side-by-side) or *carré* (facing four) — and the system allocates within availability. You can also ask to be seated next to a specific companion by giving their coach and seat. Effectively random within your stated constraints.
- **1st class:** you **pick your actual seat from a seat map** of the train, choosing coach, deck and individual seat (solo, duo facing, or carré).

Sources: <https://www.sncf-connect.com/aide/placement-a-bord-trains>, <https://www.tgvinoui.sncf/voyager/voyager-avec-tgv-inoui/classes-de-confort>

**[E] For multi-person bookings this is a real argument for 1st class on the long legs** — being able to select specific seats together, rather than hoping the allocator keeps the booking intact, may be worth the premium.

**[V] Family space:** TGV inOui offers an **"Espace Famille"** — a priority family area near the nursery corner, a **free option, bookable in 2nd class only**, and available **at weekends (Friday 14:00 to Sunday afternoon), during school holidays and on public holidays**. Source: <https://www.sncf-voyageurs.com/fr/voyagez-avec-nous/preparez-votre-voyage/voyagez-avec-un-bebe/>

**[E] Note the trade-off:** Espace Famille is 2nd class only, so it is mutually exclusive with 1st-class seat-map selection. **[E] Also flag: whether their specific dates qualify (25 Sep is a Friday; early October is not a French school holiday) needs checking at booking.**

**⚠️ Quiet coaches: unverified.** I found no current official SNCF page documenting a "quiet coach" or *espace Zen* product on TGV inOui. Do not plan around one.

### 5.8 Children's fares

**[V] Under 4:** travels **free on a lap** on TGV inOui, TER and Intercités, with no reserved seat, but **must be declared at booking**. For a dedicated seat, the **Forfait Bambin costs a flat €9**. On OUIGO, under-4s are free on a lap, or €8 (Grande Vitesse) / €5 (Train Classique) for their own seat. <https://www.sncf-connect.com/aide/l-offre-pour-les-enfants-et-les-familles>, <https://www.sncf-voyageurs.com/fr/voyagez-avec-nous/preparez-votre-voyage/voyagez-avec-un-bebe/>

**[V] Ages 4–11:** there is **no automatic child discount without a card**. The route to a discount is the **Carte Avantage Adulte**:

- **€49, valid 1 year**
- **−30% for the cardholder** on all TGV inOui and Intercités, 2nd and 1st class
- **−60% for up to 3 accompanying children aged 4–11**, provided their tickets are bought **at the same time** as the cardholder's
- The 30% holder discount applies to one-way trips **any day when travelling with a child aged 0–11**, to one-ways on Saturdays and Sundays, and to round trips including a Friday/Saturday/Sunday night

Sources: <https://www.sncf-voyageurs.com/fr/voyagez-avec-nous/en-france/tarifs-grandes-lignes/carte-avantage/carte-avantage-adulte/>, <https://www.sncf-connect.com/catalogue/description/carte-avantage-adulte>

**[S]** A secondary guide describes the no-card 4–11 discount as "≈50%" (<https://www.kombo.co/blog/fr/tarif-enfant-sncf-2026/>), but **[E]** I could not confirm a standing 50% child discount on SNCF's own pages — the official material routes 4–11 pricing through the Carte Avantage. Treat the 50% figure as unverified.

**⚠️ On "Carte Avantage Famille": [E] I found no product by that name in SNCF's current catalogue.** The relevant products are the **Carte Avantage Adulte** (above) and the separate **Carte Familles Nombreuses**, which SNCF's FAQ describes as being for families with **3 or more children**.

**[E] Does buying a card pay off here?** One €49 Carte Avantage Adulte gives its holder −30% and up to three accompanying children aged 4–11 a −60% discount when the eligibility rules above are met. **[E] Run the numbers on the actual quoted fares**, because deep-discount Prems fares may already undercut the Avantage price and the discount is calculated off full fare.

**⚠️ [V] Age proof:** SNCF requires a *livret de famille* and ID to prove a child's age. **[E] Chinese travellers generally will not have a French livret de famille** — a child visitor's passport showing date of birth should suffice, but this is worth confirming with SNCF, and each child visitor should carry their own passport on every train.

### 5.9 Luggage rules — real, recent, and enforced by a fine

This is one of the best-documented items in this report, straight from SNCF.

**[V] Standard allowance per passenger on TGV inOui and Intercités:**

- **2 labelled bags, max 70 × 90 × 50 cm each** (suitcase, large backpack, hiking or sports bag), **plus**
- **1 labelled hand baggage, max 40 × 30 × 15 cm** (backpack, tote, laptop bag)

**Alternative configuration** if carrying a special item: **1 bag (70 × 90 × 50) + 1 special item (90 × 130 × 50 — pushchair, folded scooter, instrument in a labelled case, skis/snowboard in a labelled cover, folding wheelchair) + 1 hand baggage.**

Sources: <https://www.tgvinoui.sncf/voyager/informations-pratiques/politique-bagages>, <https://www.sncf-voyageurs.com/fr/voyagez-avec-nous/en-france/tgv-inoui/vos-bagages-et-consignes/>, <https://www.sncf-connect.com/aide/transport-de-vos-bagages> (page last updated **21 August 2026** — three days before this research)

**[V] Two overriding rules:**

1. **All baggage must be labelled** (*étiqueté*). Onboard crews can supply labels.
2. **You must be able to carry all your baggage yourself, unaided, in one go.**

**[V] The fine — this is the number that was asked for:**

- **€50** for 1 excess or non-compliant bag
- **€100** for 2
- **€150** for 3 or more

Additionally, **€150** for obstructing aisles or improperly occupying a seat or luggage space. Source: SNCF Connect FAQ and both SNCF Voyageurs pages above, all consistent.

**[V] Cross-border variation:** the same rules apply on TGV inOui to/from **Spain, Belgium, Freiburg-im-Breisgau and Luxembourg**. On **TGV Lyria (Switzerland)** and TGV inOui/ICE to/from **Germany with DB**, there is **no restriction on the number or weight** of bags (you must still carry them yourself), with a max dimension of 90 × 130 × 50 cm. Italy has its own specific rules.

**[E] Is it enforced?** SNCF published a formal fine schedule with per-bag escalation and keeps the page current, which is not what an unenforced policy looks like. My honest read: **enforcement is real but discretionary** — crews are far more likely to act on bags blocking an aisle or occupying a paid seat than to audit a booking's suitcase count. I did not find enforcement statistics, so this is an inference, not a fact.

**[E] Practical implication:** the allowance is generous because it is calculated per passenger. The binding constraint is not the allowance but the physical one — *carry everything yourself in a single trip* — and the aisle-obstruction fine. **[E] Recommendation: prefer medium suitcases to enormous ones**, keep each within 70 × 90 × 50 cm and make sure each traveller can manage their own bag. And **label every bag** — it is free, it is mandatory, and it is the one rule most likely to be checked.

### 5.10 First vs second class for comfort, and where the luggage goes

**[V] What 1st class actually buys on TGV inOui:**

- **3 seats abreast instead of 4**, so wider seats
- **Reclining seats with headrest and footrest**
- **A calmer atmosphere with fewer passengers**
- **Seat-map selection at booking** (see §5.7)

Second class: reclining seats in duo or *carré* configuration, described by SNCF as "particularly suited to families and groups." Source: <https://www.tgvinoui.sncf/voyager/voyager-avec-tgv-inoui/classes-de-confort>

**[E] Recommendation:** travellers seeking lower fatigue may prefer 1st class for legs over three hours; 2nd class is usually adequate for Paris→Tours (~1 hour). The reasons are practical rather than luxurious: a wider seat and footrest over 3–4½ hours, a quieter car, seat-map selection and less passenger churn. **[E]** The differential on French domestic TGV is typically modest when booked in advance — often 30–50% over second class rather than a multiple. Verify against actual quotes.

**[V] Where luggage goes — SNCF's own instructions:**

- **Cabin-size bags:** at your feet, **under the seats**, or in the overhead rack above your seat.
- **Large bags:** in the **shared luggage spaces at the entrance to the coach, in the middle of the coach, or at the far end**, and in the floor gaps between back-to-back seat pairs.
- **On duplex (double-deck) sets, if you are seated on the upper deck, take your bags up with you** to keep them within sight.

Source: <https://www.tgvinoui.sncf/voyager/informations-pratiques/politique-bagages>, <https://www.tgvinoui.sncf/voyager/informations-pratiques/bord-de-tgv-inoui>

**[E] Two practical notes.** First, SNCF's advice to take bags to the upper deck is a warning in disguise: bags left in the downstairs rack are out of sight from an upper-deck seat. Booking the **lower deck (*salle basse*)** keeps luggage nearby and avoids stairs — useful for visitors seeking lower walking intensity. **[E] Request *salle basse* explicitly at booking.** Second, luggage space is **shared and first-come**; boarding early matters more than the class of ticket. **[V]** Boarding on TGV inOui closes **5 minutes** before departure, and SNCF advises arriving **30 minutes** ahead (<https://www.sncf-connect.com/fr-ch/tgv-inoui>) — **[E]** multi-person bookings with luggage should take the 30 minutes seriously.

## 6. Car rental in France for multi-person bookings

### 6.1 What class of vehicle

**[E]** A multi-person booking with substantial luggage may not fit in a standard 5-seat car and may not comfortably fit a compact 7-seater either, because the rearmost seats in a Grand Scénic-class MPV occupy the boot. The realistic choices:

- **7–9 seat van** (Toyota Proace Combi, Renault Trafic, VW Caravelle, Mercedes Vito class) — passengers in the first rows, luggage in the back. **[E] This is the practical category for larger bookings.**
- **[S]** Europcar explicitly lists the **Renault Grand Scenic** and **Toyota Proace Combi** at Nice Airport as carrying "up to 7 and 9 passengers." <https://www.europcar.fr/fr-fr/places/location-voiture-france/nice/nice-cote-d-azur-aeroport>

**⚠️ [E]** A 9-seat van is a large vehicle for Provençal village lanes and Riviera corniche roads, and parking in Gordes or Èze with one is genuinely unpleasant. This is a real argument for §7's private-driver option.

### 6.2 Driving licence — the critical issue, and it has a trap

**[V] French law for short stays.** Per service-public.gouv.fr, a non-EU/EEA licence is valid in France during a short stay (e.g. a holiday) provided it is valid, complies with any medical conditions noted on it, and is **either written in French, or accompanied by an international driving permit, or accompanied by an official translation**. A translation done abroad must be **legalised or apostilled**; a translation done in France must be by a **traducteur habilité** (sworn translator on a court of appeal list) **or by the issuing country's embassy in France**. Source: <https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F1459>

**⚠️ [S] The trap: China does not issue International Driving Permits.** China is not a party to the 1949 Geneva or 1968 Vienna road-traffic conventions, so no legitimate IDP exists for a mainland Chinese licence. Documents sold by third-party apps as "international driving permits" for Chinese licences are **not** IDPs. Sources: <https://worlddrivingpermit.com/driving-licence-translation/china>, <https://bestcarrentals.co.uk/en/blog/uk-chinese-license-guide>, <https://www.advantura.com/post/can-i-drive-in-spain-with-a-chinese-license-2026-tourist-guide>

**[E] So of the three routes French law allows, only one is open to mainland Chinese licence holders: an official French translation.** Get either (a) a translation by a **traducteur assermenté** registered with a French cour d'appel, or (b) a translation issued by the **Chinese embassy/consulate in France**, or (c) a translation done in China that is then **legalised or apostilled**. **[E] Option (a) is the most reliably accepted at rental counters** and can be arranged remotely before departure.

**[V] What the rental companies say:**

- **Sixt** is categorical: *"Si votre permis est rédigé en arabe, chinois, japonais, cyrillique ou tout autre script non latin, vous devez présenter un PCI valide avec votre permis original. Aucune exception."* Elsewhere on the same page it allows *"un permis de conduire international (PCI) **ou une traduction officielle**"* for non-EU/EEA licences in non-Latin script. <https://www.sixt.fr/help-center/articles/quelle-anciennete-de-permis-faut-il-pour-louer-chez-sixt/>
- **Hertz:** *"Si le permis n'est pas rédigé en alphabet latin, un permis de conduire international (PCI) peut être exigé."* Also requires the licence held **at least 1 year**. <https://www.hertz.fr/p/location-de-voiture/france>
- **Carrefour Location** accepts either an international permit **or** a **traduction certifiée conforme** by an authorised translator or the issuing country's embassy in France. Also: **minimum age 21**, licence held **at least 3 years**, ID, and a payment card in the driver's own name. <https://location.carrefour.fr/bien-louer/conduire-en-france-avec-un-permis-etranger>

**⚠️ [E] The gap is Sixt's "Aucune exception" wording versus the fact that no Chinese IDP exists.** Taken literally it would make Sixt unrentable for Chinese licence holders. Their own text elsewhere permits an official translation, so this is probably sloppy drafting — but **[E] travellers should not gamble a €1,000 prepaid rental on which reading a specific counter agent takes.** Concrete advice: **email the specific branch in advance**, in writing, describing exactly what will be presented (original Chinese licence + sworn French translation + passport + credit card in the driver's name), and **carry the printed reply to the counter**.

**[V] Also required at pickup, universally:** original licence (**no photocopies, no photos, no digital images**), passport, and a **credit card in the main driver's name** — the same card used for the booking. Any additional driver must appear in person with their own licence. Sources: Hertz, Sixt and Europcar pages above.

**[S]** And note the police-side risk separately from the rental-side risk: The Local France points out that even where a rental company does not ask for the translation, *"if you are pulled over, the French police officer will expect to see one of these two documents."* <https://www.thelocal.fr/20251014/reader-question-can-i-rent-a-car-in-france-with-a-foreign-driving-licence>

### 6.3 Costs, deposit and transmission

**[S] Daily rate:** for a 7-seat MPV/van in France, roughly **€80–90/day** typical, with promotional rates from about €30/day and airport-location premiums pushing higher. This is aggregated and imprecise. Nice Airport rental desks: Avis, Budget, Europcar, Hertz, Enterprise, Goldcar and Sixt, all at the **Car Rental Center behind car park P5, Terminal 2**. <https://www.nice.aeroport.fr/services/location-de-voiture>

**[E] Budget €90–140/day for a 7–9 seat van in late September / early October**, plus one-way drop-off fees if collecting and returning in different cities. Late September is shoulder season on the Riviera, which helps.

**[V] Deposit / *caution*:** taken as a **pre-authorisation hold on a credit card**, not a debit — funds are frozen, not charged, and released at the end of the rental. **The amount varies by vehicle category and location** and neither Sixt nor Europcar publishes a single figure. <https://www.sixt.fr/location-voiture/france/nice/aeroport-nice-cote-azur/>, <https://www.europcar.fr/fr-fr/places/location-voiture-france/nice/nice-cote-d-azur-aeroport>

**[E] For a 9-seat van, expect a hold of €1,000–2,500.** The credit card must have enough available limit to absorb the hold on top of trip spending, and it must be a true credit card in the driver's name. **[E] Debit cards are frequently refused for large vans** — verify with the branch.

**[V] Franchise (excess/deductible):** the rental includes a damage excess which you can buy down. **Avis** markets *"l'assurance tous risques Avis Inclusive… zéro franchise"* as an add-on. <https://www.avis.com/fr/locations/eur/fr/nice/aeroport-de-nice-cote-d-azur-t2>

**[E] Standard excess on a large van in France is typically €1,000–2,000, with zero-excess cover adding €15–35/day.** For a driver unfamiliar with French roads, narrow Provençal village streets and tight underground car parks, **[E] I would buy the zero-excess cover** — the expected value of a scraped bumper is high and the alternative is arguing about a €1,500 charge from Shanghai.

**⚠️ [E] Automatic transmission: this is a bigger issue than most Chinese travellers expect.** Most Chinese drivers licensed in recent years drive automatic only, while the **default in the European rental fleet is manual**. Automatics are a **separate, scarcer, more expensive booking class**, and a booking that does not explicitly specify automatic will produce a manual at the counter. **[E] Expect a €10–25/day premium and book the automatic explicitly and early** — automatic 7–9 seaters are the scarcest combination in the fleet. I could not obtain published automatic-surcharge figures from the rental companies, so this range is an estimate. **[E] Also check whether the Chinese licence carries an automatic-only restriction**, since French law requires compliance with restrictions noted on the licence **[V]** (service-public F1459, cited above).

### 6.6 Crit'Air and low-emission zones (ZFE) in 2026

**[V] The legal status was genuinely in doubt this year and is now settled: ZFEs remain in force.** France's parliament voted in April 2026 to abolish the ZFE obligation as part of the *loi de simplification de la vie économique*, but on **21 May 2026** the **Conseil constitutionnel**, in decision **n° 2026-903 DC**, struck down the relevant article (Art. 37) as a *cavalier législatif* — an amendment lacking sufficient connection to the original bill under Article 45 of the Constitution. The censure was **procedural, not substantive**, so ZFEs revert to their prior legal framework and a future dedicated bill could revive abolition.

Sources: <https://www.flotauto.com/zfe-conseil-constitutionnel-decision/>, <https://lcp.fr/actualites/zfe-le-conseil-constitutionnel-censure-la-suppression-des-zones-a-faibles-emissions>, <https://www.bfmtv.com/economie/entreprises/transports/les-zones-a-faibles-emissions-sont-retablies-le-conseil-constitutionnel-censure-la-suppression-des-zfe-votee-en-avril_AN-202605210733.html>, <https://theconversation.com/zfe-de-la-suppression-au-sursis-une-saga-juridique-qui-na-pas-encore-dit-son-dernier-mot-284844>

**[V] The Crit'Air sticker requirement therefore stands.** It applies to **all vehicles including foreign-registered ones**, and the fine for driving in a ZFE without a valid visible sticker starts at **€68** for cars (€135 for vans/commercial vehicles). Enforcement is by number-plate camera. Sources: <https://www.rac.co.uk/drive/news/motoring-news/law-change-for-uk-drivers-in-french-cities/>, <https://www.europermits.co.uk/blog/2026-crit-air-sticker-guide-uk-drivers>, <https://francestickers.co.uk/guide/critair-hired-car>

**[V] For a French-registered rental car, the rental company is responsible for obtaining and displaying the sticker** — but **the driver is ultimately responsible for it being there**, and any fine will be passed on by the rental company. Practical protocol: **check the windscreen at pickup and photograph the sticker.** <https://francestickers.co.uk/guide/critair-hired-car>
