# Loire Valley, Provence & French Riviera — sightseeing logistics research

**Trip dates:** 30 September – 6 October 2026
**Research date:** 24 August 2026
**Party:** 5 people — 2 adults (~40), 2 seniors (~70), 1 child (~7)
**Bases:** Tours or Amboise (30 Sep – 1 Oct) · Avignon (2–3 Oct) · Nice (4–5 Oct) · fly home from Nice via Paris 6 Oct

Every price and opening time below is sourced. Anything I could not confirm on an
official site is marked **[UNVERIFIED]** or **[ESTIMATE]**.

## 0. Days of the week — read this first

The itinerary lands on a set of weekdays that collide with several French closure
patterns. This matters more than any single ticket price.

| Date | Day | Where | What it collides with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 Sep | Wednesday | Loire | Chambord equestrian show is **between seasons** |
| 1 Oct | Thursday | Loire | Beauval shuttle bus does **not** run (weekday, no school holiday) |
| 2 Oct | Friday | Avignon | — |
| 3 Oct | Saturday | Avignon | Fanfares & Bodega street festival in Avignon that evening |
| 4 Oct | Sunday | Avignon → Nice | **First Sunday of the month** = several museums free; L'Isle-sur-la-Sorgue market day (but it's their transfer day) |
| 5 Oct | **Monday** | Nice | Antibes Picasso Museum **closed**; Cours Saleya is the **antiques** market, not the flower/food market; Monaco's Prince's Palace **is** open (it's closed 1–4 and 7 Oct) |
| 6 Oct | Tuesday | Nice → home | Musée Matisse **and** Musée Chagall both **closed Tuesdays** |

Verification of the weekday arithmetic: 1 January 2026 is a Thursday, and 1 October
is exactly 273 days later (273 = 39 × 7), so 1 October 2026 is also a Thursday.

### The three highest-impact findings

1. **Chambord now charges €31 per adult to visitors who are not nationals or residents
   of the European Economic Area.** From 14 January 2026 the château runs an
   experimental two-tier price: €31 reference rate, €21 for EEA nationals/residents on
   proof. For a Chinese family that is 4 × €31 = **€124** for the two adults and two
   seniors (the 7-year-old is free). Source:
   https://www.chambord.org/fr/preparer-ma-visite/horaires-et-tarifs/
2. **The Chambord horse-and-raptor show does not run on their dates.** The 2026 season
   is 4 April – 27 September, then 17 October – 1 November. Their Loire days
   (30 Sep–1 Oct) fall in the gap. Same source as above.
3. **Monday 5 October is their only full Riviera day, and it is the right day for
   Monaco.** The Prince's Palace is closed 1–4 October and again on 7 October, but open
   on the 5th. Meanwhile Antibes' Picasso Museum is shut on Mondays and Nice's two big
   museums are shut on Tuesday the 6th.

# A. LOIRE VALLEY

## A.0 Base: Tours vs Amboise

**Recommendation: Tours**, with a caveat.

The deciding fact is that **Amboise to Chenonceaux is not a direct train**. SNCF Connect
lists 9 services a day, all with one change (usually at Saint-Pierre-des-Corps), average
1 h 12, fastest 49 minutes. From Tours the same trip is **direct**, 24–30 minutes,
9 services a day (first 06:54, last 20:53).

| Leg | From Tours | From Amboise |
|---|---|---|
| Chenonceaux | Direct, 24–30 min, 9/day | 1 change, 49 min – 2 h 16, 9/day |
| Blois-Chambord (for the Chambord shuttle) | ~35–40 min **[ESTIMATE]** | Direct, fastest 14–18 min |
| Saint-Aignan–Noyers (for Beauval) | Direct, 41–59 min, ~12/day | Not verified |
| Château d'Amboise & Clos Lucé | Train, ~20 min **[ESTIMATE]** | On foot |

Sources: https://www.sncf-connect.com/train/horaires/tours/chenonceaux ·
https://www.sncf-connect.com/train/horaires/amboise/chenonceaux ·
https://www.thetrainline.com/en/train-times/tours-to-st-aignan-noyers ·
https://www.virail.fr/trains-blois-amboise

Tours also has far more hotel and restaurant choice and connects to the TGV network at
Saint-Pierre-des-Corps. Amboise wins only on atmosphere and on having two paid sights
within walking distance of each other.

**Practical shape for 2 nights in Tours:** Chenonceau on one day (direct train), Amboise
town on the other (château + Clos Lucé, both walkable from Amboise station). Skip
Chambord — see A.1 for why.

### Buy the Pass Rémi Découverte

**A single Pass Rémi Découverte covers up to 5 people for unlimited travel on all Rémi
trains and coaches in Centre-Val de Loire: €45 for 2 consecutive days, €60 for 3 days.**
One ticket for the whole group; the offer is capped at 2 passes per family. That is €9
per person for two days, and it covers the Chambord and Beauval shuttle coaches as well
as the trains. It also carries discounts at partner sites including Beauval and the
châteaux.

Source: https://www.ter.sncf.com/centre-val-de-loire/tarifs-cartes/billets-reduction/pass-remi-decouverte
· https://www.remi-centrevaldeloire.fr/billets-et-offres/pass-remi-decouverte-2-jours

**Verdict on Chambord for this family:** the combination of €124 in entry fees for four
adults, no equestrian show, a lot of stairs, and a car-free day built around two bus
departures from a town they aren't staying in makes this the weakest of the Loire
options. Chenonceau delivers more for less effort.

## A.2 Château de Chenonceau — the best Loire choice for this family

Official site: https://www.chenonceau.com/ ·
prices: https://www.chenonceau.com/infos-pratiques/tarifs/ ·
hours: https://www.chenonceau.com/en/practical-information/opening-times/

**2026 individual prices (with brochure):**

| Category | With brochure | With audioguide (on site) |
|---|---|---|
| Adult | **19.00 €** | 24.00 € |
| Senior (65+) | **16.00 €** | 21.00 € |
| Student (with card) | 16.00 € | 21.00 € |
| Child 7–18 | **15.00 €** | 20.00 € |
| Child under 7 | Free | not available |

Family offer (from 3 paying children aged 7–18): 15.00 € per family member. This family
has one child, so it does not apply.

**Cost for this party: 2 × 19 + 2 × 16 + 1 × 15 = 85 €.** Chenonceau is one of the few
Loire châteaux with a real senior rate.

**October hours:** 28 September – 1 November, **9:00 to 17:30**. Those times are the
ticket-office last-access times; the monument closes 30 minutes later and the gardens a
further 30 minutes after that.

**Why it works for a 7-year-old:** the château is built across the river Cher on
arches, so the whole thing reads as a bridge-castle — visually striking in a way a child
registers immediately. There are **rowing boats on the Cher, 5 € per person, daily**,
which is the single easiest "fun thing" in the Loire for this trip. The gardens (Diane
de Poitiers and Catherine de Médicis parterres) and the maze are flat and stroller-
friendly. Source for the boats: https://www.chenonceau.com/infos-pratiques/tarifs/
(visitor-information banner)

**Train from Tours:** direct TER Tours → Chenonceaux, 24–30 minutes, 9 services a day
(first 06:54, last 20:53). The line runs along the Cher valley. **Chenonceaux station is
400 m from the ticket office** — about a 5-minute walk down an avenue of plane trees.
Sources: https://www.sncf-connect.com/train/horaires/tours/chenonceaux ·
https://www.chenonceau.com/infos-pratiques/plan-acces/

Note: frequency is not hourly outside peak summer, so pick the return train before
setting out. The Chenonceaux station building is small and unstaffed outside core hours
— buy tickets in advance or load them onto the SNCF app.

**Booking:** at the time of research the site was displaying "high attendance:
reservation of a time slot compulsory" for the August peak. **[UNVERIFIED whether timed
slots remain compulsory in October]** — book online anyway, it is free to do so.

**Is Clos Lucé worth it for a 7-year-old? Yes — more than any other Loire château
interior.** The park contains 40-odd full-scale working models built from Leonardo's
drawings, which children are allowed to touch and operate, plus translucent
reproductions of the paintings. It is an outdoor, hands-on site rather than a
walk-through-the-rooms site, which suits both the child and the seniors' walking pace.
The trade-off: at €20 an adult it is more expensive than the royal château next door,
and the park has gravel paths and some slopes.

**Doing both in one day** costs 2 × 17.30 + 2 × 20 + 11 + 15 = **€109.60** for the
family (assuming no transport discount), and is a comfortable full day from Amboise
station on foot. Clos Lucé is 400 m from the royal château.

This is a genuinely time-limited draw, and for a Chinese family the panda-diplomacy angle
may or may not be a selling point — they can see pandas at home. Weigh that honestly
before spending a whole Loire day and two taxi legs on it.

**How long:** the park is 45 hectares with 35,000 animals. Official advice and every
guide agree that **one full day (9:00 to closing) covers the essentials and two days is
recommended with children**. With two 70-year-olds and a moderate walking pace, one day
will be tiring; the "Nuage de Beauval" cable car is included in the entry price and
saves a long walk.

weekends and during school holidays**, in the window 4 April – 1 November 2026 (plus
19 Dec – 3 Jan). **30 September and 1 October 2026 are a Wednesday and a Thursday
outside the school holidays (the Toussaint break starts 17 October), so the shuttle does
not run on either of their Loire days.**

Shuttle times when it does run: Blois 09:15 → Beauval 10:15; Beauval 17:20 → Blois
18:20. Fare 3.40 € each way.

Sources: timetable PDF

**Is Beauval realistic as a car-free day trip on their dates? Only with a pre-booked
taxi at both ends.** Two 8 km taxi legs for five people, plus a 45-minute train each
way, plus a full day on foot in a 45-hectare park, on one of only two Loire days. It is
doable but it consumes the day completely and leaves no margin. Cost is also the highest
of any Loire option (~€190–210 in entry alone).

**My read:** if the pandas are the draw, Beauval is worth the day and the taxi bookings,
because January 2027 ends giant pandas in France. If the child would be equally happy
with the Chenonceau rowing boats and the Clos Lucé machines, skip Beauval — those two
cost a fraction and fit the seniors' pace far better.

## A.5 Other family activities worth pre-booking

### Hot-air balloon over the châteaux
Flights operate **April to October**, at sunrise or late afternoon; about 1 hour aloft
and 3–4 hours in total including inflation, the 4×4 retrieval and the certificate
ceremony. Departures from Amboise, Chenonceaux, Chaumont-sur-Loire and Loches depending
on wind.

Indicative 2026 prices, adult, per person: 195–223 € (the lower "weekday" ticket is
Monday–Friday, morning flights, excluding public holidays); child under 12: 165–169 €;
a family/friends rate of about 199 € per person applies from 3–4 tickets.

**Minimum age 6 and minimum height 1.30 m** so the child can see over the basket edge,
and under-12s must be accompanied. **Check the 7-year-old's height** — 1.30 m is tall
for a first-grader and may well be disqualifying. Flights need wind under about
15 km/h, so cancellations are common; tickets are valid 18–24 months.

Sources: https://www.airtouraine.fr/en/montgolfiere/vol-en-montgolfiere-chateau-de-la-loire/vol-en-montgolfiere-chateau-de-la-loire/
· https://montgolfiere-sensation.com/acheter-vol-en-montgolfiere/vol-en-montgolfiere-a-amboise-envol-des-chateaux-de-la-loire-indre-et-loire-37/
· https://www.tourainevaldeloire.com/offres/amboise-montgolfiere-chenonceaux-amboise-fr-5151891/

With two 70-year-olds this needs thought: operators require passengers to be able to
step over the basket wall and hold a bent-leg landing position for about 10 seconds.

### Troglodyte caves — Cave des Roches, Bourré
A working mushroom farm 50 m underground in former tuffeau quarries, plus a carved
"underground town". Guided visit, 1 hour, in French and English.

Official site: https://www.le-champignon.com/visite-guidee/

- **Prices:** adult 12 €, child under 16 **7.50 €**, under 7 free. No booking needed for
  individuals. Group rate (20+, booking compulsory) 10 €/7 €.
- **From 1 October to 11 November:** guided visits at **11:00, 15:00 and 16:00 only**
  (the April–September schedule has six daily slots). Arrive 10 minutes early.
- Constant 12 °C underground — bring a layer.
- **Cost for this family: 4 × 12 + 7.50 = €55.50.**

Bourré is near Montrichard, in the Cher valley between Chenonceaux and Saint-Aignan.
**[UNVERIFIED: public transport access to Bourré. Assume a car or taxi is needed.]**

This is a good low-effort, weather-proof option, and mushroom caves land well with
children. The narrow October window (three fixed departure times) means it has to be
planned around, not dropped in.

Source: https://donneespubliques.meteofrance.fr/FichesClim/FICHECLIM_84007005.pdf
(statistics 1993/1994–2020) · mirrored at
https://www.meteociel.fr/obs/clim/normales_records.php?code=84007005

**What this means in practice:** pleasant days in the low twenties, cool mornings around
10–11 °C. But **October is one of Avignon's two wettest months** (88.6 mm, second only to
November's 92 mm), and Mediterranean autumn rain arrives as short violent downpours
rather than drizzle — the October daily record is 136.2 mm, and October 2000 delivered
205.8 mm in the month. Pack real rain gear, not umbrellas, and keep one indoor option in
reserve (the Carrières des Lumières is ideal).

**The Mistral:** a cold, dry, north-westerly that blows down the Rhône valley roughly
**100 days a year, with gusts to 100 km/h**. The **maximum recorded October gust at
Avignon is 87 km/h**. It clears the sky and drops the apparent temperature sharply — a
21 °C day in a Mistral feels like 14 °C. It also makes the exposed ruins at Les
Baux-de-Provence and the top of the Pont du Gard genuinely unpleasant, and it is the main
reason hot-air balloon and boat operators cancel.
Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climat_de_Vaucluse (citing Météo-France)

the monthly mean was 20.9 °C with a range of 19.0–22.5 °C. The Mediterranean lags the
air by about a month, so early October water is warmer than the water in June.
Source: https://meteo-quotidien.com/france/nice/sea_temperature/october
**[Third-party sea-temperature data. Directionally reliable — 20–22 °C in early October
is consistent across sources — but not an official measurement.]**

Nice's beaches are pebble, not sand, which matters for a 7-year-old and for bare feet.
Water shoes are worth packing.

Note that October is also a month of heavy episodic rain on this coast — the October
rainfall record at Nice is 182.4 mm in a single day (2000).

## D.4 French restaurant hours — the thing that will actually catch them out

This is the most likely source of a bad evening on the whole trip, and it is entirely
avoidable.

### Kitchens close between services

French restaurants are **not** open continuously. The standard pattern is:

- **Lunch: 12:00–14:00** (some to 14:30 at weekends)
- **Dinner: 19:00–22:00** (some to 22:30 or 23:00 at weekends)
- **Between about 14:30 and 19:00 the kitchen is shut.** Arriving at 15:30 hungry, or at
  18:00 with a tired child, means no hot food at a proper restaurant.

Sources: https://resto-resa.com/budget-bons-plans/restaurant-a-proximite-ouvert/ ·
https://www.saveursdupaysdesaintmalo.fr/restaurant-ouvert-lundi/

### Typical hours by restaurant type

| Type | Mon–Thu | Fri–Sat | Sunday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bistrot | 12:00–14:00 / 19:00–22:00 | 12:00–14:30 / 19:00–23:00 | **Closed** or 12:00–15:00 |
| **Brasserie** | **11:30–23:00 continuous** | 11:30–00:00 | **11:30–23:00** |
| Pizzeria | 12:00–14:00 / 19:00–22:00 | 12:00–15:00 / 19:00–23:00 | 12:00–15:00 / 19:00–22:00 |
| Asian restaurant | 12:00–15:00 / 19:00–23:00 | 12:00–15:00 / 19:00–00:00 | 12:00–15:00 / 19:00–22:00 |
| **Gastronomique** | 12:00–14:00 / 19:30–22:00 | 12:00–14:30 / 19:30–23:00 | **Closed** |

Source: https://resto-resa.com/budget-bons-plans/restaurant-a-proximite-ouvert/
**[Third-party aggregate figures, not official. Directionally correct and consistent with
the other sources, but treat the percentages below as indicative.]**

### Sunday and Monday closures

- **Sunday is the harder day.** Roughly **55 % of brasseries open Sunday lunch but only
  30 % Sunday evening**; pizzerias 40 % lunch / 20 % evening; Asian restaurants 35 % / 25 %.
  Traditional bistrots and gastronomic restaurants are the most likely to be shut all day.
- **Monday is the classic French closing day** for independent restaurants — many close
  Sunday evening *and* all day Monday, because the markets and suppliers are quiet.
  Brasseries, pizzerias and neighbourhood restaurants are the reliable Monday options.
- **The reliable categories, in order: brasseries (continuous service, open every day),
  then pizzerias, then Asian restaurants, then anything near a station, hospital or
  tourist area.**

### What this means for this specific itinerary

- **Sunday 4 October, Avignon → Nice, arrival evening in Nice.** Sunday evening is the
  single worst dinner slot of the week. **Book in advance.** Nice's Vieux Nice is a
  tourist area, which helps, but do not arrive at 20:30 hoping.
- **Monday 5 October, Nice, after a full day in Monaco.** Monday evening is the second
  worst. **Book in advance**, or target a brasserie.
- **Peak dining time is 19:30–20:30.** For a quieter room and faster service, sit down at
  **18:30–19:15** — which also suits a 7-year-old's bedtime — or after 21:00.
- **Booking:** for anywhere good, phone or book online at least 48 hours ahead. About
  30 % of restaurants that open on Sunday will not take a last-minute telephone booking.
  TheFork and Zenchef have "open Sunday" filters and give instant confirmation.
- **Fallbacks that are almost always open:** brasseries (11:30–23:00 continuous),
  covered markets (Avignon's Les Halles is open daily except Monday), bakeries for lunch,
  and supermarkets for a picnic. Nice has the additional advantage of *socca* and
  street-food stands in Vieux Nice.
- **Prices for orientation:** a *menu du jour* at lunch runs about €12–25; a brasserie
  dinner €20–40; a gastronomic dinner €30–70 per person.

### One more habit worth flagging
Lunch is the cheap meal in France. The weekday *formule* or *menu du jour* at lunch is
often half the price of the same kitchen's dinner. For a party of five, eating the main
meal at lunch and something light in the evening is both cheaper and better matched to a
child's and a senior's energy curve.
